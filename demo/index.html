Parallax.

Learn to read X-ray views

Practise reading X-rays: compare two views of the same wire, then see in 3D where it really lies.

Working out where a wire really sits from two flat X-ray views is hard to learn from films alone. Made for junior trauma and orthopaedics trainees.

Explore in 3D Try a lesson Source code and notes on GitHub

Before you use it

The X-ray images are worked out from a 3D model. They haven’t been checked against real clinical views.

Your answers stay in this tab. Nothing is saved except whether you prefer light or dark.

See it working

Parallax in 3D Explore. Select it to look closer.

What you can do

  • Move the beam yourself

    Pick the femur or a simpler starter scene, change the angle of the X-ray beam, and look around from any side.

  • Test your eye

    In the lessons, compare the images and choose where you think the wire is, with a reason, before it’s shown in 3D.

  • Start simple

    The starter scene has three short challenges about where a wire sits, before you move on to the femur.

What it won’t do

It’s for teaching and exploring, not clinical use or judging real films.
