About this lesson

Two cases explore how a guidewire and femoral shaft fracture appear on different X-ray views. Your answer is compared with the model, not graded as a diagnosis. Getting the answer right does not prove that the images were enough to decide. Two reassuring views are not a universal rule for safety.

Illustrative anatomy and instrument limits

The femoral shape comes from the Pat059 dataset. The short section of shaft canal is an approximation, not a confirmed endosteal boundary. Its ends are artificial caps, not anatomical closures. The 16 mm transverse fracture gap and 10 mm posterior shift of the distal fragment were added for this lesson, not inferred from CT.

Only a short section of an olive-tipped guidewire around the fracture is shown, not a complete instrument or insertion path. The wire has a fixed shape and rounded tip. It is not a model of a particular device or how it bends during insertion. Missing the distal canal through the fracture gap does not mean the wire has pierced intact cortex.

Images and focused 3D detail

The AP reference was made in advance. A new X-ray view is calculated after you stop moving the C-arm. Orbital rotation and angulation change the beam direction. Rotating the image does not change the beam. The image area and brightness scale stay fixed across cases and views. Saved images do not change: they keep the view and image rotation you saved. The overview always hides the wire. The close-up 3D view shows it only after you confirm your answer and shows just the area around the fracture, not the full femur. Looking around the 3D scene does not change the X-ray view or saved images. These are synthetic images, not clinical X-rays.

This is a public teaching demonstration. Its anatomy and clinical accuracy have not been validated, and its effectiveness for learners has not been established. It gives no placement advice.
