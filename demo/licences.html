About & licences

Parallax is a projection-geometry teaching lab using simplified models. Not for clinical use. The models are not anatomically validated and the lessons have not been tested with learners.

The rig does not model machine travel or collisions. One view, or a pair that looks reassuring, is not a general test of containment.

Responses reset when you refresh. Only your colour-theme preference is saved. There are no accounts, uploads or analytics.

How the X-ray images are made

Each image is calculated through the 3D model, not taken as a screenshot of it. For every pixel, Parallax traces a straight line from the X-ray source to the detector and measures how far it passes through bone or wire. A longer path through material, or material that blocks more of the beam, makes that pixel brighter. Structures at different depths can overlap in one view and separate in another.

The whole-femur explorer’s outer shell is set to block more of the beam than its inner core; its Uniform option treats the whole bone as one material. The separate CT-informed proximal-femur lesson uses a heuristic shell and interior envelope with weak, smoothly varying derived density. Its wire adds stronger opacity. All views use the same brightness scale, without automatically adjusting the contrast of each image.

3D Explore and both femur pages calculate new projections in your browser when you move the beam. The guided projection-fundamentals lesson uses images calculated in advance. Looking around the 3D scene only changes your viewpoint; it does not change an acquired image.

The images leave out soft tissue, scatter, the range of X-ray energies, blur and detector noise. Pixel edges can shimmer. These simplifications help show the geometry, but do not reproduce all the features of a clinical X-ray.

Femur lesson

This lesson uses a CT-informed proximal-femur model, not the whole-femur BodyParts3D model in Explore. The 105 mm-long, 1 mm-radius wire represents a planned screw, with its tip standing in for the screw tip. Its 130° guide angle is relative to a measured local shaft direction, not clinically registered anatomy. Fixed wire positions and model-defined AP and lateral views still need expert review; the lateral view makes the neck look shorter.

The tip-to-marker distance is not clinical TAD. It measures to the same gold surface marker in both images and adjusts for magnification using the known depth of each endpoint. Dimensions are model millimetres, not independently validated anatomical measurements. The 33 mm head envelope used to check framing is not an anatomical head radius. The 25 mm combined-distance limit and 8 mm centring limit are rules for this exercise, not clinical grading rules.

Baumgaertner et al., 1995 studied lag-screw tip–apex distance and cutout in sliding hip-screw fixation. Its 25 mm finding is a teaching reference, not a guarantee of safe fixation. The indexed abstract was inspected, not the complete paper. This lesson does not assess fracture reduction, implant selection, stability or overall fixation suitability.

CT-informed proximal femur

The femur lesson derives from the Norm_02 subset of Dual Fluoroscopy In Vivo Pelvic and Hip Joint Kinematics in Patients With Cam Femoroacetabular Impingement Syndrome, by Andrew E. Anderson, Penny R. Atkins, Niccolo Fiorentino, Rich Lisonbee, Jared Zitnay and Lindsay Schuring. Published 2025-09-30 by The Hive: University of Utah Research Data Repository. The derived outer surface, interior envelope and density retain Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported, not MIT. No endorsement by the source authors or repository is implied.

Parallax converted coordinates and rasterized the surface, reconstructed a restrained interior envelope, and masked, smoothed and quantized CT-derived density. Source CT resolution is approximately 0.685547 × 0.685547 × 1 mm; resampling to 1 mm adds no acquisition resolution. Raw CT is not bundled. See attribution and changes and source identifiers, hashes and methods.

Anatomical status remains partial. The imposed 1.8 mm inset and heuristic envelope are not measured cortical thickness or an accurate tissue segmentation. The density is not resolved trabecular anatomy or calibrated attenuation. Artificial end caps and source truncation remain. Sampled entry-rim checks do not certify the continuous entry disk; the continuous interior-course bound excludes the first 3 mm entry collar. Numerical checks do not establish clinical safety or learner benefit.

The source package reproduces projections from the supplied meshes and keeps the earlier procedural model’s numerical evidence separately. None of these models has anatomical or clinical approval.
